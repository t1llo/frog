# Design rules

Frog looks quiet and consistent: Raycast's dense, keyboard-first popups, and a settings window in the manner of macOS System Settings and the Rex terminal: a sidebar panel floating inside the window with back and forward beside the window's buttons, the page's title in a toolbar on the page's content edge, and borderless cards whose rows are set apart by space. Every value comes from [DesignSystem.swift](../native/FrogApp/Views/DesignSystem.swift), [CompactControls.swift](../native/FrogApp/Views/CompactControls.swift), [Motion.swift](../native/FrogApp/Views/Motion.swift) and the v2 components in [Views/Design](../native/FrogApp/Views/Design). A view never invents its own button, dropdown, radius, height, text size, material or animation. `StyleTokenScanTests` checks the rules that code can check.

[DesignGallery](../native/FrogApp/Views/Design/DesignGallery.swift) shows every component. After changing one, draw it and look: `FROG_PAGE_SHOTS=<folder> swift test --filter 'FrogAppTests.PageShotsTests/testDesignGallery'` writes `design-gallery-light.png` and `design-gallery-dark.png`.

## Less text

- A page has a title and at most one line of subtitle. A section has a caption and at most one sentence of footer.
- A row has a title (a noun: "Shortcut", "Model") and at most one short caption. Longer explanations go in `.help()`.
- Say a thing once: not in the subtitle, the caption and a footer.
- **One warning per problem.** A page or sheet says what is wrong once. Where a `ProblemNotice` (or `ModelMissingNotice`) says it, the control that fixes it carries no second mark: its title asks ("Choose a model"), and the notice's button opens it (`RuleModelPicker(marksMissing: false)`). Without a notice, the control is marked once: `FrogDropdown`'s `.warning("Reason")` swaps its symbol for the warning triangle, a row the dropdown is not in gets one `ProblemMark`, or the row's caption says why in the warning colour. Never a triangle beside the control and a caption under it as well.
- Name things the same everywhere: "Previous display", "Add rule", and coding tools "use their own sign-in" (not "login").
- **Full stops follow the grammar, not the slot.** A caption, subtitle, footer or message that is a sentence ends with a full stop: it has a verb of its own, a statement ("Frog installs it when it quits.") or an instruction ("Hold this shortcut to see every rule and its keys."). A fragment does not: a noun phrase, a list or a label, even with a clause inside it ("Shell scripts that run with zsh in your home folder", "Up to 200 MB each", "1.6 GB · WhisperKit"). Text of more than one part ends every part with one ("Kept in memory. Passwords and keys never are."). Titles, buttons, badges, menu items and tooltips that name an action never have one.
  - A feature's or plugin's summary (`PluginInfo.summary`, a directory entry) is a tagline: a short phrase without a full stop, even when it starts with a verb ("Speak instead of typing"), in Features, the command bar and setup alike. A command bar row's subtitle is a label in the same way ("Shows the answer in a panel"). A summary from outside Frog loses its trailing full stop where it joins a `·` caption.

## No lines in the chrome

- Cards are not split into rows by lines: `FrogSection` sets its rows apart by their height (`rowHeight`) and a little space, so every card on every page looks the same whether its rows are `FrogRow`s or a page's own.
- No `Divider()`, no `NSMenuItem.separator()`, and no thin `Rectangle` above footers, beside sidebars or anywhere else.
- Separate everything with space, a heading, or a surface: a card, an inset fill, or the footer band.
- A popup's footer sits on `frogFooterBand()` (`FrogStyle.footerBand`, a shade darker than the panel), never under a line: `PanelFooter`, `FrogPopupFooter` and any footer a popup draws itself.
- Exceptions: the user's own content (Markdown rules and quote bars), chart axes and gridlines, progress tracks, and the drop indicator while dragging.

## Radii

Corners nest. A shape inside another has the outer radius minus the gap between them, so both curves share one centre and the gap stays even all the way round. Every row, field and highlight is `controlRadius`; each container is that plus the gap its content keeps from its edge, and the tokens are defined that way in `FrogStyle`, so they cannot drift apart.

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `smallRadius` | 4 | Key caps, code chips, colour swatches: marks inside a control |
| `controlRadius` | 10 | Fields, rows and their highlight, menu rows, symbol tiles, blocks inside a card or popup, a popup's search field |
| `cardRadius` | 14 = 10 + `listInset` 4 | Cards, settings groups, lists, previews, banners; a list's rows sit 4 in |
| `panelRadius` | 18 = 10 + `panelInset` 8 | Popups and floating panels (`frogPanel()`); their search field, rows and blocks sit 8 in, and a card inside one sits `panelCardInset` (4) in |
| `windowRadius` | 12 on macOS 26, 10 before | The main window's own corner, drawn by macOS. Frog links against the macOS 14 SDK, so macOS 26 draws its legacy 12-point continuous corner, not the 26 SDK's larger one (measured on the capture) |
| `sidebarRadius` | 8 = 12 - `sidebarInset` 4 | The main window's sidebar panel, concentric with the window's corner; its rows sit `sidebarRowInset` (14) in, so their content stays 18 from the window's edge |
| `pillRadius` | 18 | 36-point status pills (`frogPanel(pill: true)`), drawn as true capsules: a continuous corner of half the height flattens the round ends |

Buttons, search boxes, segmented controls and switches are capsules, as in Rex; so are badges (`FrogBadge`), status pills, dots, avatars, progress bars and skeleton bars. No other numbers. Cards, controls and the sidebar panel use the `.continuous` corner style.

## Sizes and spacing

- Choices and text (`FrogDropdown`, `CompactSegments`, fields, search boxes) are `controlHeight` (28) tall in the window and in sheets; in popups and panel headers they pass `compact: true` and are `compactHeight` (24).
- **One button height.** Every button is `compactHeight` (24), lettered in `bodyMedium` with 12 either side, wherever it stands (the one exception: inside a 36-point notice pill or the answer panel, `.frog(small: true)` is `FrogButtonStyle.smallHeight` (20) with `label` text, so its curve shares the pill's centre): a page header's Open, a row's Keyboard Settings, a sheet's Save and Cancel, a popup's Retry, an `IconAction` or `IconMenu`, the add buttons. `FrogButtonStyle` fixes it, so no call site picks a size; its `compact:` is kept for old call sites and changes nothing. A button beside a field goes with the field's row, not its height. Segmented controls in the window are one size too: never `compact: true` outside a popup or panel header.
- Pages: `FrogPage` (padding `pagePadding` 20, sections `sectionSpacing` 30 apart, a column at most `pageWidth` 720 wide; `FrogStyle.wideWidth` 1080 for dashboards and tables) with a `FrogPageHeader` (title, optional one-line subtitle, actions on the trailing side). The column starts `pagePadding` from the leading edge on every page, whatever its width: a wide page only reaches further to the trailing side. A page never adds its own horizontal inset.
- **One page header.** Every page and step inside a page has one `FrogPageHeader`: the title, one line of subtitle under it, and the actions on the trailing side, level with them. In the main window all three go up to the toolbar, as a Mac window shows a title and subtitle: the title (`pageTitle`) level with the window's buttons and the subtitle (`small`, muted) under it, the actions (24 tall like every button, `FrogAddButton` among them when the page adds things) ending at the column's trailing edge. The header then takes no room in the page (`FrogPageStack` gives it no spacing), so the first section starts right under the toolbar. Never replace the subtitle with chips or an empty row: filters go in the page, under the toolbar. A sheet draws the same header itself, its title in `title`. A sub-page (History's entry, the plugin Directory, Models' tabs) shows a breadcrumb in the same `pageTitle` size, the parent muted.
- **Tabbed pages** (Models, Community plugins, History's filters): the tabs are `CompactSegments` leading the first row under the toolbar, in a `ListToolbar` when the tab has a search. The page keeps one subtitle for every tab; what a single tab needs to say goes inside that tab (a notice, a section footer). The toolbar shows at most the one add action of the tab that is open.
- **Two edges in the main window.** The toolbar's title and subtitle, a page's filters and search, and its cards start on the column's leading edge. Everything that reads as text of a section starts `rowInset` (14, the card padding) in: the section's heading, the rows' text inside the card and the footer under it, so a section reads down one line, as in System Settings. Nothing else is a third edge.
- The main window: an empty `NSToolbar` makes the title bar 52 points tall, so the window's buttons sit inside the sidebar panel, as in Rex. The sidebar panel is `sidebarWidth` (212) wide, floating `sidebarInset` (4) inside the window with `sidebarRadius`, a thin layer of ink over the window's canvas (`FrogStyle.sidebar`, 3 % light and 4.5 % dark), so in every theme it reads a step lighter than the page in dark and a step darker in light, never a darker hole; back and forward are two plain chevrons on the trailing side of its top strip, level with the window's buttons (⌘[ and ⌘]). Beside it a toolbar `toolbarHeight` (60) tall holds the open page's header: the title's line centred on the window's buttons, 26 points down, as a Mac window sets its title, the actions centred on the same line, and the subtitle hanging under the title. `FrogPageHeader` hands its title (`FrogPageTitleKey`), subtitle (`FrogPageSubtitleKey`) and actions (`FrogPageActionsKey`) up to it; `FrogPage` hands up its width. A sheet is its own window, so its header sets `.environment(\.frogToolbarTitle, false)` and draws its title.
- A section is `FrogSection("Heading", footer:)`: a heading (`FrogSectionHeader`), one card, an optional one-sentence footer. Inside it every setting is a `FrogRow("Title", caption:) { control }`, at least `rowHeight` (40: a 28-point control and `space6` above and below, so dropdown, segment and switch rows are one height) tall, its title in `row` (13), its text `rowInset` (14) from the card's edge. Rows are set apart by space, not lines.
- **Headings.** Every group on a page or in a sheet has the one heading style, `FrogSectionHeader` (`section` 13 semibold, the text colour, `rowInset` in, trailing actions in `small` muted ending where the rows' controls end). A page that groups its own content (a list, a grid) puts a `FrogSectionHeader` above it, the first card included.
- **When a page gets headings: the card rule.** Settled once; reviews do not reopen it. Count the cards a page (or a tab, or a sheet) shows in its usual state, with optional rows folded away:
  1. **One card, no heading.** When everything a page sets fits in one card, it is one `FrogSection()` without a title: the page's title already names it (Stay awake, Hidden bar).
  2. **Two or more cards, every card headed.** Each heading names what sets its card apart from the others ("Rules" and "Selection bubble", "Apps" and "Actions"; Explain and Command bar each show two headed cards, what opens it and how it answers or what it finds), never one headed and one not.
  3. **No one-row card beside another card.** A card that would hold a single setting row joins the card nearest it in meaning instead; a row that only appears with another (a folder under "Save answers to notes") stays in that row's card. The one exception is a page's only card.
  4. **Split only by kind or length.** A new card starts where the content changes kind (settings, a list of the user's things, a graph, a preview, a grid of tiles) or where one card would pass about eight rows. A list of the user's things (rules, apps, entries, models) is one card whatever its length, even one row, since its length is the user's.
  5. **A heading never repeats the page's or tab's name** ("Dictation" on Dictation, "Installed" under Installed), and a card's heading is never its only row's title.
  
  `FrogSection("Heading")` and `FrogSection()` are the two forms; `StyleTokenScanTests` can't count cards, so a reviewer checks the rule against the page's picture.
- **One counter form**: a count belongs to its heading as `FrogSectionHeader("Apps", count: 23)` (`FrogSection(count:)`), the number muted in tabular `row` digits after the title, read as "Apps, 23"; never "Apps · 23", "(23)", "23 apps" or "4 of 23" in the title. - `SectionCaption` follows where it is: inside a card or a popup it is the small muted caption of a sub-group; outside one it draws as the section heading, at the same inset. Never add your own padding to a heading.
- **A step inside a page** (a History entry, Statistics inside Settings, an app under review in App cleaner, a community plugin's page under Community plugins) is `.frogPageBack(id, parent: "History") { … }`: the toolbar shows a breadcrumb, "History › Proofread", whose first part goes back, and Back and ⌘[ take the step before the previous page. That is the only back pattern: a page draws no "‹ History" link of its own and never binds ⌘[. A sentence that belongs to no row is a `FrogRowNote`; other content (previews, button rows) goes in a `VStack(spacing: 10).padding(.vertical, 10)`.
- **The one row recipe.** Every row in a card starts its icon or text `rowInset` (14) in from the card's edge and ends its trailing control `rowInset` in, so icons, titles and switches line up down the window whatever page they are on. There are two ways to get there and no third: a `FrogRow` inside a `FrogSection` (the section supplies the inset), or a list whose rows highlight on hover, which is a card padded `listInset` (4) holding rows padded `rowPadding` (10) on both sides (4 + 10 = 14). A page never pads a row with its own number. Menus follow the same split inside their cards: `MenuActionRow` and `StayAwakeMenuRow` pad `rowPadding` inside a `listInset` card, so the switch ends where the keycaps do.
- Cards in a popup or a menu tab are `panelCardGap` (4, the same as their `panelCardInset` from the panel) apart in every menu (Frog, System, Usage, the LLM usage and System monitor popups). Every tab puts its short status on the footer's leading side (`MenuContribution.status`: System's "Live", Usage's "16m ago"), and its header row leads with its controls.
- `FrogRow(…, icon: { PluginIconTile(…) })` puts a feature's icon before the title.
- A caption that is a file path is `FrogRow("Model folder", caption: .path(url.path))`: one line, home as "~", cut in the middle, the full path on hover. A row about a file (a failure in App cleaner) is `FrogRow(path:caption:)`, the path as its title the same way. A caption that names a command is `caption: .attributed(InlineCode.attributed("Run `frog plugin publish` …"))`. No page rebuilds `FrogRow`'s layout by hand for these.
- v1 names, for pages not moved yet: `PageScroll` and `PageHeader` now draw `FrogPage` (wide) and `FrogPageHeader`; `SettingsSection` with `CompactRow` (card padding 14, rows 10 apart) still works. Move a page to `FrogSection` and `FrogRow` all at once, never one section of it.
- Inside a card, a sub-group starts with a `SectionCaption`.
- The sidebar always shows the open page's row: Models, Features and Settings are pinned under the scrolling list at every window height, and opening a plugin's page from anywhere scrolls the list by the least amount that brings its row into view (Statistics shows Settings).
- List rows are 1–2 apart, highlighted with `frogRowHighlight(hovering:selected:)`, or `frogHoverRow(selected:)` when the row tracks its own hover.
- Spacing comes from the `FrogStyle.space2`…`space16` scale (2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16), never an odd value such as 7 or 9: 2 between a title and its caption, 4 inside a label, 6–8 between controls in a row, 10 between blocks inside a card, 12 between a row's icon, text and control, 16 between groups in a popup.
- One content edge per surface, so text lines up from the header to the footer:
  - Cards, section cards, popup headers, popup bodies and `PanelFooter` start content `cardPadding` (14) from the edge.
  - A list is inset `listInset` (4) and its rows padded `rowPadding` (10): 4 + 10 = 14, the same edge. A search or query line above the rows gives its icon the rows' icon width and gap, so the query starts where the titles do.
  - Sheets and setup use `sheetPadding` (20) on every side, header, body and buttons alike; the onboarding window uses `pagePadding` (20) like a page.
  - A ghost button that starts a line (a back link, Skip setup) puts its text on the content edge with `.padding(.leading, -10)`; only its hover fill reaches past it.
  - A ghost button in a section header's accessory (Refresh, Clear, Select all) ends its text on the trailing edge by itself: `FrogSectionHeader` sets `frogTrailingGhost`, and the button pulls its own padding past the edge. Call sites add no negative padding.
- In a sheet, `FrogPageHeader`'s title is a control tall, so it sits at the same height whatever the subtitle or actions; actions are 8 apart. Icon actions in a page header are plain (`IconAction`, `IconMenu(bordered: false)`).
- The sidebar: the window's buttons, a `SearchBox` that narrows the rows, Frog's own row (a 32-point `FrogMark`, "Frog" and a status line; it opens Settings with ⌘, and says which permission setup still needs), then the plugin groups and Community pages, which scroll, and Models, Features and Settings pinned at the bottom. Each plugin group is headed by its name (`SidebarGroupHeader`: `label` semibold, muted, `sidebarHeaderHeight` 18, 6 in like the rows' tiles, as Finder's sidebar heads Favorites and Locations), and set apart by one `sidebarGroupGap` (10); Frog's row has no header; the pinned Models, Features and Settings are headed **Frog**, so no rows sit unheaded under the last group. The search field follows the strip that holds the window's buttons directly, 12 points under them. Community plugins and the pages plugins add share one group, **Plugins**, every plugin page with the community tint, so nothing a plugin adds floats unheaded at the list's end. Headers are rows of the grid, so the scroll rests on them like on any row, and the last row is never cut; at the default window size (1000 × 760, `MainView.defaultSize`) the features Frog starts with and the ones setup recommends fit without scrolling. When they do not (most features on, Phone, community plugins, a smaller window down to the 800 × 520 minimum), only the list scrolls: Models, Features and Settings stay pinned under it at every window height and on every page, so the main destinations never scroll away or hide. The list's scroll rests only on a row's top edge (`SidebarRowGrid`, `SidebarRowSnap`), and revealing the open row scrolls on to the next row edge, so a row never shows cut in half under the search field: the list keeps one group gap under the field (`sidebarTopFade`), its top row rests just under that gap, and a row scrolled up fades out through it (`topScrollFade`, `sidebarVisibleTop`), never cut hard at the field; above the pinned rows a row that would be cut is held back (`sidebarVisibleBottom`) until the scroll brings all of it into view, and a group header is held back with its first row, so the list always ends on a row boundary. A held-back row is never blanked and never cut hard: it shows as content slipping under the edge, at most 80 % strong on its side towards the list and fading to nothing at the edge (`SidebarGridRow.ghostOpacity`), the way a Mac sidebar's rows slip under its search field; a row scrolled up past the gap under the field fades the same way. So the edges themselves say the list goes on, with no band, shade or line drawn over the gap; a held-back row is hidden from VoiceOver, and clicking it opens its page, which scrolls it whole into view. The open row is always revealed one group gap clear of the pinned rows. `SidebarScrollTests` holds this at the minimum size. Rows are `sidebarRowHeight` (28) tall with a `PluginIconTile(.sidebar)` and the title in `row`; the open row is filled with the neutral `sidebarSelection`, never the accent.

## Type

Use `FrogType` for text. A literal size is only for an SF Symbol's size. **Minimum sizes**: nothing is set under 11 (`caption`, `label`), and a sentence someone reads, a row's caption, a description, a footer's status, is at least 12 (`small`). A footnote that repeats its row's text is cut rather than shrunk. The scale is three clear steps: a page title (20) above a sheet title (17) above a section heading (13 semibold), so a page never reads as a sheet and a section never as a page.

| Token | Use |
| --- | --- |
| `hero` 26 bold | The welcome headline in setup, the one hero line |
| `pageTitle` 20 bold | The open page's title in the main window's toolbar, and every sub-page's breadcrumb, at the same size |
| `title` 17 semibold | Sheet titles, setup step titles, standalone panels (shortcut reference, window cheat sheet), big figures |
| `row` 13 | Row titles in section cards, sidebar rows |
| `rowMedium` 13 semibold | The sidebar's Frog row |
| `section` 13 semibold | Section headings, popup titles, empty-state titles |
| `lead` 13 | Sheet subtitles, empty-state messages |
| `body` 13 | List items, field text, dropdown values, menu rows |
| `bodyMedium` 13 medium | Buttons, titles of cards and settings |
| `small` 12 | Row descriptions and captions, secondary lines, compact controls, footer status, a page's subtitle in the toolbar |
| `label` 11 medium | `SectionCaption`, key caps, footer hints |
| `caption` 11 | Timestamps, paths, counts, badges, footnotes |
| `mono` 11 | Code, file contents and identifiers only. Not shortcuts |

## Buttons

`FrogButtonStyle` is the only button. The main window sets the secondary kind for every button in it.

- `.buttonStyle(.frog(.primary))`: the one main action of a page, sheet or card, filled with the accent and lettered in `onAccent` (white, or the theme's dark surface on a pastel accent).
- Secondary (default): every other action.
- `.frog(.destructive)`: removes or deletes something, a grey pill lettered in `critical`.
- **Three looks, no fourth**: primary, secondary and destructive are the whole set in the window, sheets, popups and rows. A cancel, a Clear, a Rename or a Refresh in a header is a secondary pill; a remove that is one of many per row is an `IconAction` (`xmark`).
- `.frog(.ghost)` is kept only for the setup assistant's way-finding links (Back, Skip, Skip setup, Import settings…): the label in `ink` with no fill until hovered. Nowhere else; `StyleTokenScanTests` keeps it there.
- **Disabled**: one look for every kind, the secondary pill with its fill at `disabledOpacity` (0.6) and its label in `muted`, so it still says what it would do; a disabled primary drops its accent, a disabled ghost stays plain muted text. **Live or disabled is told by the label's tone**: an enabled button's label is always `ink` (or `onAccent`, `critical`), a disabled one's always `muted`. So a ghost is never muted at rest, or Skip beside a disabled Continue reads as two dead buttons. Never fade a label further. **A disabled action says why next to it**, never only in a footer or a tooltip: `.frogDisabledShowingReason("Load a model first")` puts the reason, a muted fragment without a full stop, on the button's leading side in a row or header (Unload now), or the row's caption says it in a row that has one (Review under Install from a link). In a sheet the reason takes the footer's leading note instead (Check source, Run), so it never lands between Cancel and the primary. `.frogDisabled(_:)` (tooltip and VoiceOver hint only) is for a control whose reason already shows on the page. Never `.disabled(true)` alone on an action. Switches, checkboxes and dropdowns fade by the same `disabledOpacity`. A setting that only matters while its parent runs (Stay awake's duration and Keep the screen on, while no session runs) rests at the same fade and stays changeable, its caption saying it is for the next time, so an on switch never claims something happens while nothing runs; the page's header then shows the parent's state as a `FrogStatePill` either way.
- One size: every button is 24 tall, in rows, headers, sheets and popups alike (see Sizes and spacing); the small size (`.frog(small: true)`, 20) is only for buttons inside a notice pill or the answer panel.
- **Adding something** is `FrogAddButton("Add rule")`: a secondary pill with a plus, in the page header (or a section header for a section's own list). When the user first picks what to add ("Add app"), the same pill with a chevron (`FrogAddLabel(menu: true)`), which only a list has, opening a searchable `FrogDropdownList`, as Shortcuts' add buttons do. Never a bare "+ New", a popup-menu text button or an icon alone.
- **Sheet buttons** are `FrogSheetButtons { destructive } trailing: { FrogCancelButton { }; FrogPrimaryButton("Save", disabledReason:) { } }`: Delete (or Remove) alone on the leading side, Cancel next to the primary on the trailing side, the primary last. Escape presses Cancel, Return the primary. Back in a multi-step sheet goes between Cancel and the primary. `FrogSheetButtons(padded: false)` drops its own `sheetPadding` when the sheet already pads its content, as the small Rename and Try sheets do.
- `IconAction` for icon-only actions: a plain icon that highlights on hover. `bordered: true` only in a popup header, beside compact segments, and in a menu-bar menu tab's header row, which is that popup's header in the menu (refresh and open in Usage are boxed in both).
- `IconMenu` for a menu behind an icon ("…" by default): drawn exactly like the `IconAction` beside it. Never `.menuStyle(.borderlessButton)`, which drops Frog's surface and hover.
- `.buttonStyle(.plain)` only for rows, tiles and cards that draw their own hover with `frogRowHighlight`.
- Never `.bordered`, `.borderedProminent`, `.borderless` or `.link`.

## Controls

- **Every single choice is a `FrogDropdown`.** Never `Picker`, a `Menu` used as a picker, `CompactMenu` or a hand-made popover list. It is generic over a `Hashable` value: `FrogDropdown(selection: $value, options: values, title: { $0.title }, symbol: { … })`, or `selected:`/`choose:` when the choice is saved through an action. For an optional choice make the value optional and offer `nil` ("System default", "No model").
  - Sections and rich rows: `sections: [FrogDropdownSection("Downloaded", values: …)]` with `item: { FrogDropdownItem(title, detail:, symbol:, badge:, disabled:) }`.
  - Long lists (models, apps, microphones, languages; anything over about 8): `searchPrompt: "Find a model"` turns on the search field on the same list. Search matches title and detail.
  - `compact: true` in popups and panel headers; `width:` for the button (220 by default); `.buttonTitle { … }` when the button should say less than the row; `.disabled(…)` fades it by `disabledOpacity`; `.warning(reason)` marks it as the control that fixes a problem (the triangle in `warning`, the reason as its tooltip).
  - The list opens on the chosen row. Arrow keys move, Return chooses, Escape closes; the highlight never animates.
- Choices of two to four, and switching between parts of a page (Providers, Installed tools): `CompactSegments`, a capsule whose chosen segment is filled with the accent. Segments are as wide as their labels.
- Steps that follow each other in a sheet (a new provider: Service, Connection, Models) are numbered steps joined by a short track, not segments: the open step's number filled with the accent and its name in `bodyMedium`, a done step a tick, a later one muted and not clickable until it can be reached.
- Choices that are best seen, such as the colour scheme, Classic or Glass, and the theme: tiles with a picture and the name under it, the chosen one ringed in the accent (Appearance settings). The pictures are sketches of a window, nothing else drawn on them. Appearance is one card (scheme and style, Theme under a `SectionCaption`, accent), so the themes are in reach without scrolling past a second heading.
- `IconMenu` is for actions behind an icon (Rename, Delete, Add an app), not for choosing a value.
- On/off settings: `Toggle` with `.toggleStyle(.frogSwitch)`: a 36 by 20 capsule with a round white knob 2 in. On fills it with `switchOn`, the accent; off is the neutral `switchTrack`. While the window is not in front, and while it is disabled, an on switch is a darker grey than off (never a pale wash of the accent), and a disabled switch fades. A setting that can still be changed is never dimmed: dim only what is `.disabled`. `FrogRow` applies it to its control, and the row's title names it.
- Picking items from a list (files to remove, models to offer, agents to set up): `.toggleStyle(.frogCheckbox)`. Never the system checkbox.
- Numbers from a short list: `FrogDropdown` rather than a `Stepper`. A free whole number is − field +, the field `FrogStyle.numberFieldWidth` (64) wide whatever its bounds, so the minus and plus of rows above each other line up.
- Text: `LabeledField` or `frogControlSurface()`, with a `FrogTextField(placeholder:text:)` inside: it draws the placeholder in `FrogStyle.placeholder`, a step lighter than muted, where a bare `TextField` draws it nearly as dark as a value. No `.roundedBorder`.
- **History** groups entries under day headings (Today, Yesterday, a weekday within the week, then the date), and each row shows its time of day in the Mac's clock ("09:41" or "9:41 AM"), never a relative age.
- **History's diff** works by word: the letters that changed are marked strongly (added in `good` and underlined, taken out in `critical` and struck through) and the rest of each word they sit in lightly, as a code review marks a line and the characters in it, so one changed letter still reads as part of its word.
- **Rules look the same everywhere**: `RuleIconTile(rule:)` on the Writing page, in the selection bubble (`RuleIconTile(item:)`) and on the command bar's rule rows. The tile's colour says what the rule does, from the glyph its name gives it: proofreading green, translating blue, email orange, defining and explaining teal, summing up purple, shortening pink, polishing indigo; a name that says nothing known wears its feature's colour. A translation shows its language's two-letter code ("EN", "DE") instead of a globe. A rule row's caption says what the rule does with the result ("Replaces the selection", "Copies the result", "Shows the answer in a panel"), so every row keeps one height.
- **Features** list every feature with its summary; one that will not work yet keeps the summary and shows a quiet trailing "No model" mark (the warning triangle and the words in `small`, in `warning`) beside its switch, the whole reason on hover. Its page says how to fix it.
- **Statistics** puts each card's count in its heading (`FrogSection("Rule runs", count:)`), as every counted group does, and writes numbers in English digits with commas. A metric tile is its icon and name on top and the figure large under them.
- **Scripts**: the script fills the editor card, as a note does, and its output folds out in an inset panel right under it, its caption showing the first printed line while shut. It opens by itself after a run and then stays as the person leaves it.
- **Window switcher keys**: the keys the panel answers to are a grid two to a line, caps then the word, as a cheat sheet sets them, never a column of full-width rows that read as settings.
- **Notes tokens** (`{{date}}`, `{{time}}`, `{{clipboard}}`) draw as small rounded chips reading "date", "time", "clipboard", in the accent when the note fills them in and muted when it pastes them as written; the editor and the preview never show raw braces.
- **Search has one place**: the row directly above the list it filters, as a `ListToolbar`: filters or tabs leading, the `SearchBox` trailing at `ListToolbar.searchWidth` (220); with no filters it fills the row. Never in the page header, the toolbar or beside an add button. In a list-and-editor page (Notes, Scripts) it heads the list column. `showsSearch: false` hides the field while the tabs keep their place. The sidebar and dropdown lists keep their own field. Filters that narrow a list (All, Text, Audio): `FilterTag`.
- **Shortcuts are caps, everywhere**: `KeyCaps("⌃⌥Space")` or `KeyCaps(hotkey:)`, one 18-point `KeyCap` per key, in the sans font: in rows, sheets, footers, menus, lists, layout tiles and the shortcut reference. A recorded shortcut that can be changed is a `HotkeyRecorder`: its caps on a faint hover, clicked to record again. One that can be cleared keeps a clear button (`xmark.circle.fill`) beside its caps, shown only while the pointer is over the recorder or it records, and offered to VoiceOver as an action; a required shortcut (Command bar, Clipboard history, Window switcher, the Shortcuts list) passes `showsClearButton: false`. Only while recording (or before anything is recorded) does it draw a pill ("Press shortcut…", "Record shortcut"). `ShortcutBadge` is the same caps for a shortcut shown where it cannot be changed. Never glue a shortcut's glyphs into one chip or write it as plain text: `KeyCaps(["⌥", "N"])` for a list of keys, and `KeyHintLine(text:)` for a hint written as text ("⌥Space to stop · Esc to cancel"), which draws each shortcut as caps. Bare `KeyCap(` is only for one key. **Many empty slots: the quiet recorder.** Where a list has a slot for a shortcut on every row and many stay unset (the Windows page's layouts, Writing's rule rows, any list of three or more recorders), the recorder is `HotkeyRecorder(…, unset: .placeholder)` and the row is marked `.frogRevealsRecorder()`: a set shortcut shows its caps; an unset one rests as a faint dash (`–` in `placeholder`) on the same trailing edge, and turns into the "Record shortcut" button while the pointer is anywhere over the row, over the recorder, or the recorder has keyboard focus (Tab with keyboard navigation on), so it stays reachable and VoiceOver still reads it as "Shortcut: Record shortcut". The button keeps its room while hidden, so nothing moves when it shows. Never a wall of identical buttons, and never a footnote explaining the dash. A single recorder in a row of its own keeps the default `.button` look. The section header counts how many are set as "4 of 23 set" in muted `body` digits. Writing's rule rows record in place: a rule's shortcut is its recorder in the row, never a sheet opened to set it. A list that shows shortcuts it cannot change (the selection bubble) keeps the column with the same faint dash for a row without keys. The shortcut reference groups by feature, named as the sidebar names it: Writing, Explain, Dictation, Windows (window layouts and their rules), then Frog. A group that would hold one row naming its own page (a lone Explain under Explain) joins Frog's own shortcuts instead. Window layout tiles in the picker fill each row's width (the three thirds one row, the two two-thirds the next, no empty cells), and a row reserves the keycap slot on every tile once any of its tiles has a shortcut, so a row's tiles are one height and their labels line up, while a row with no shortcut stays compact. A layout has one name on every surface (`WindowAction.title`: "Top left", "Left two-thirds", "Maximize", "Centered 50%", "Next display", "Restore"), on the Windows page, in the picker, the cheat sheet, the shortcut reference and the command bar.
- Waiting: `FrogSpinner` is the one spinner (not a bare `ProgressView()`): eight spokes in `ink` fading round, Frog's own drawing because the system's indicator was too faint on dark panels; `FrogLoadingNote(text: "Looking for apps…")` is a spinner and one muted line inside a card; values still being measured are `FrogSkeleton` bars of about the value's width, so nothing moves when they arrive. Say "Measuring…" once for a group at most, never per value. A download or removal with a known share done is a determinate `ProgressView(value:)`. Skeleton bars use `FrogStyle.skeleton`, a step above the control fill so they show on a light card. Accent words on a card or `accentSoft` ("Recommended", an accent `FrogBadge`) use `FrogStyle.accentText`, the accent eased until it reads at 4.5:1. A chart's scale labels stand in a column left of the plot (`FrogChartScaleGutter`), each centred on its gridline, never on the plot.
- **Empty lists and pages: one recipe**, `FrogEmptyState`: a 40-point tile, a `section` title, one `lead` sentence and the action that fills it, unless the page header already has that action, 32 points of space above and below, in its card. Whatever belongs to a feature (its page, its list, a feature that is off) passes the feature's `tint:` so the tile is the feature's coloured tile with a white symbol; only what belongs to no feature ("No matches", a load that failed) keeps the neutral `SymbolTile`. Never a bare glyph, a lone grey line in a card, or a card taller than its content. When a switch is what fills the page (empty Statistics with Record statistics off), the switch is the empty state's one control, under its sentence, never a separate card beside it.
- **Warnings: once per page, with the fix.** A problem that keeps a page's feature from working is one `ProblemNotice` above its sections, always with the button that fixes it (`ModelMissingNotice` opens the flagged model picker via `modelPickerOpenRequest`, or Models when there is nothing to choose). The control at fault is marked once, inside it (`FrogDropdown(warning:)`). Rows get their own mark only to single out some of them, never when every row is affected. A sheet never repeats in its footer what a control in it already marks.

## Surfaces and colour

- Cards: `FrogCard`, or `frogTableSurface()` for lists that draw their own rows. A card is a thin layer of the text colour over the page (`FrogStyle.card`, 3.5 % light and 7.5 % dark) with no border, so it lifts off the page in every theme, dark ones included; controls are a step above (`control`, 6 % / 12 %), meter and progress tracks use `track` (8 % / 13 %) so they show on a card, Increase Contrast adds an outline. Blocks inside a card use the inset fill with `controlRadius` and no border.
- Controls (buttons, fields, search boxes) are filled with `FrogStyle.control`, one step above a card, and have no outline unless Increase Contrast is on. A dropdown is Rex's: the value in plain text and a small round button with an up-and-down chevron. In a row its knob, like a recorder's last cap, ends on the content edge level with switches and buttons; only the hover fill reaches past. `.fitToContent()` makes the button only as wide as its value, so a label sits right against it (Scripts' Time limit); never `.fixedSize()` around a dropdown.
- Popups and floating panels are the window's own canvas colour with a hairline edge; their footer sits on the footer band, a shade darker, with no line.
- **Sheets are the page.** Every sheet's root ends in `frogSheetSurface()`: the main window's canvas over its material, so its cards, controls and buttons have the colours they have on a page. Never the system's sheet grey, `panelSurface` or a material of its own.
- **One footer**, `FrogFooter { status } actions: { FrogFooterHint("Paste", keys: "↩") }`, for every popup, panel and menu: the status on the left (muted `small`, one line, it keeps its room before the actions do; a live status is `FrogFooterStatus(text:tone:)`, a 6-point dot in its tone before the text: `good` for Live or fresh, `active` while working, `warning` for stale or needing attention, a hollow ring for `off` while measuring or with no reading), the actions on the right as ink words each followed by its caps, on `frogFooterBand()`. The status is laid out first; where the row is too narrow for both, each hint drops its word and keeps its caps (the word stays in its tooltip), and only a status longer than the whole row truncates. The Notes and Scripts editor card ends in a plain row on the card's own fill instead (when it was edited, then the item's own controls ending on the content edge), as its bar along the top is; its title heads its text, renamed in place. Status that belongs to the open content ("Updated 3 min ago", "Live", a count) lives here; a menu tab hands its status up through `MenuContribution.status`: Frog's "Ready", System's "Live", Usage's "16 min ago" (the oldest reading, every account in the tooltip). A footer status is never cut. Frog's menu has one footer on every tab at its fixed width: "Settings ⌘ ," with its caps, then "Quit" as its word alone (⌘Q still quits and is in its tooltip), so no tab's status squeezes Settings to bare caps. A chart's scale labels are written one way per chart: a middle label written another way than the top ("$2.50" between "$0" and "$5") is left out, its gridline kept. **Every launcher and panel footer reads the same way**: on the left the surface's icon and name, a count muted after a `·` ("Clipboard · 6 items", "Window switcher · 23 windows"); on the right the primary action with ↩, Actions ⌘K where there are actions, and Close esc (Back esc inside a command's argument), in that order. Cards end 8 above the band (`panelCardInset` on the other sides), in popups and in Frog's menu alike. `PanelFooter` (keyboard panels) and `FrogPopupFooter` (status item popups) are this footer with fixed actions.
- Wool, Pewter, Deep Sea, Inkwell, Polar Night and Eggshell follow palettes of the Rex terminal: the page darkest, the sidebar panel a thin ink layer above it, cards a step above that.
- Popups: `frogPanel()`, rows `MenuActionRow` or `MenuRowStyle`, footer `PanelFooter`.
- Every `FrogPage` fades its bottom edge (`FrogStyle.pageBottomFade`, 32, which is also the room under the last card), so a card running past the window's edge fades out instead of being cut through.
- A scroll view whose content runs on below its bottom edge fades that edge with `bottomScrollFade()`, so a cut-off row reads as more to come. The fade is the hint: no "More below" button or pill is laid over the edge. One that scrolls under something pinned above it fades its top edge with `topScrollFade()` as well; the sidebar fades both edges too but rests only on row edges, so at rest nothing shows in either fade, hides a row that would be cut at either edge, and marks each edge with rows beyond it with its soft band. Every sheet with a scrolling body wraps it in `FrogSheetScroll(maxHeight:)`: as tall as its content up to the limit, then it scrolls and fades `FrogSheetScrollMetrics.fade` (32) above the sheet's buttons, so no footer line or row is cut in half there. Content that fits shows no fade and no scroll bar. Never a fixed `frame(height:)` around a sheet's body. No view draws its own fade mask.
- **Shortcut panels** (the shortcut reference, the window cheat sheet) are one card under the panel's title: each group a `SectionCaption` inside it, each shortcut a 30-point row (`menuRowHeight`) with its mark, title and caps; the mark is a window layout's picture, or the coloured `PluginIconTile(.sidebar)` of the feature the shortcut belongs to (Writing green, Command bar indigo, a plugin the community tint), the same tile it wears everywhere else, through the shared `ShortcutPanelLayout`, `ShortcutPanelCard`, `ShortcutPanelCaption` and `ShortcutPanelRow`. No one-row cards, no subtitles on single rows. A key cap that shows one glyph is exactly square, so ⌃⌥↩ lines up with ⌃⌥→.
- A plugin's popup is the template too: the web page draws its card with Frog's card fill (`--frog-card` on a `body::before` layer, so the canvas stays clear and no band shows under it) and `PluginPopupShell`'s footer names it "Community plugin".
- **The status item popup template** (System, LLM usage and every popup under a menu-bar item): `FrogPopupHeader(symbol:tint:title:)` on top, the feature's `PluginIconTile` and name in `section`, its actions (compact segments, `IconAction(bordered: true)`) on the trailing side, 40 tall; then the body's cards `panelCardInset` in; then `FrogPopupFooter { status }`, the one footer with Close `esc` on the right. A keyboard panel (command bar, clipboard history, window switcher, layout picker) uses `PanelFooter` instead, which names the panel and what Return and ⌘K do.
- **Command bar rows** each have a tile and a muted kind on the trailing side, from `CommandRowKind`: Page (Frog's own pages), Command, Application, Window layout, Clipboard, Note and so on; no row goes without one. A feature's command is named by what it does ("Show window list"), never by the page's name, so it never looks like the page beside it.
- A rule's row in the command bar wears its owner feature's tint and says that feature as its subtitle (Writing, Explain, Dictation); a window layout says "Windows". Window rules are not listed as rules, because each is already a layout row: one thing, one row. With nothing typed the bar suggests a mix, never one kind: two rules, a command, a layout, then pages, five in all (`CommandBar.starterSuggestions`).
- **Keyboard panels search on a borderless line**: the command bar, clipboard history, the window switcher and the layout picker draw no field box, only a 15-point magnifier in the rows' icon column and the query in `FrogType.query` (15), a step above the rows, as Raycast does (`FloatingPanelSearchLine`). Arguments stay chips on the control fill. The window switcher never takes the keyboard, so its line shows what is typed as text ("Type to search windows" until then), with ⌫ as a cap.
- **The panel family** (command bar, clipboard history, window switcher) is one window in three modes, as Raycast's views are: one width (`PanelFamilyPlacement.width`, 680), at most one height (460: the command bar and clipboard history are this tall, the switcher grows with its rows up to it), the same search line on top and the same footer (`FloatingPanelLayout`). Each hangs from one point, the centre of its top edge, on the display under the pointer: by default centred, with two fifths of the free height above the tallest panel and three below, so the search line opens in the same place whichever panel it is, and a list that grows or shrinks moves only the bottom edge. Every panel is dragged by its search line's magnifier, the line's empty room and its footer band (`panelFamilyDragHandle`); the family moves together, remembered per display in `panel-placement.json` beside Frog's data, so it reopens there after relaunching, and is kept wholly on screen. Dropped within 24 points of the default place it snaps home and forgets the spot; while it stands elsewhere the search line ends in `scope` ("Move back to the default place"). Clipboard history shows the list on the leading two fifths and the selected item in full on a card beside it: its text, a swatch for a colour, and its type, characters, words or lines and when it was copied. The command bar's footer leads with Frog's mark instead of the magnifier its search line already shows.
- The dictation popup's footer is a `FrogFooter`: where the words go ("Stop in Notes") on the leading side, then Stop (or "Release to stop" while a held key records) and Cancel `esc` as `FrogFooterHint`s. It shows no second Stop button or hint line.
- Badges: `FrogBadge(text: "Recommended")`, a caption in an 18-point capsule, accent-tinted when `active`; `tone: .warning` with a small symbol for a caution (Caution beside a plugin permission that reaches the user's data). **Status is never a pill.** A state (Connected, Installed, Allowed, Running, Until 14:00, a page's on/off beside its header) is `FrogStatusLabel("Connected", tone:)`: a 7-point dot and the word in muted `small`, with no fill or outline, a control's height so it lines up with switches and buttons; the dot's `tone` says the kind: `.good` green (granted, connected, working), `.active` the accent (under way), `.warning`, `.critical`, `.off` a hollow ring. Filled pills are only buttons. `FrogStatePill(text:on:)` is the old name, now drawn as this label. A `FrogBadge` names a kind or a fact about an item (App Store, Recommended, Caution), never a live state. A command to type is a `FrogCodeChip` (mono, copies on click); commands inside a sentence are `InlineCode.attributed("Run `frog plugin new`")`.
- Backgrounds: `frogSurface(.window | .sidebar | .popover | .hud)`, the one place that picks a material. Never `NSVisualEffectView`, `.background(.ultraThinMaterial)` or `glassEffect` in a view.
  - Opaque (transparency 0, the default, or Reduce Transparency / Increase Contrast): a plain fill, nothing blurs.
  - Translucent: the kind's `NSVisualEffectView` blur under the theme's colour, the same strength for every kind, so one setting looks the same on the window and every popup. A floating panel adds a light sheen at its top and a brighter top edge (`glassSheen`, `glassEdge`), like frosted glass. Liquid Glass (`glassEffect`) is not used: it changes its brightness with what lies behind, so panels with the same setting looked different.
  - `.popover` adds only the tint, because the popover brings its own material.
  - One surface per popup, on its root; rows get a plain highlight, never glass.
- **Menu-bar items and their pickers.** Hidden bar's arrow, divider and double divider share one stroke (`OrganizerMark.stroke`, 1.5) and colour, the dividers one height (`OrganizerMark.dividerHeight`, 12) snapped to whole pixels; the Hidden bar page's preview draws the same marks (`OrganizerMark.dividerImage`), never text glyphs. Stay awake's icon is its cup centred by its ink on half points; inside the ring it is the plain cup (`cup.and.saucer.fill`), so the steam never blurs at that size. A style picker (`MenuBarDesignTiles`) shares each row's width evenly, up to four cards, more than four in rows of equal count, so no card stands alone; each card is a `MenuBarPreviewStrip`, the item at its real size on a 24-point menu-bar band over a hint of the desktop, and the chosen one has the accent ring and its name in ink, semibold.
- Feature and plugin icons: `PluginIconTile(symbol:tint:size:)`, a white symbol on a tile filled with the feature's colour, `.sidebar` 20, `.list` 28, `.header` 40. Pass the feature's own `PluginTint` wherever the feature appears (sidebar, Features, setup, menu-bar popups, command bar), so it is the same tile everywhere. A tint without a colour of its own (a grey theme's accent) is drawn as a neutral tile, the control fill with an ink symbol, never a pale square that glares in dark; a pastel is deepened until the white symbol reads. Neutral, decorative symbols stay `SymbolTile`, drawn with the same proportions and corners as a `PluginIconTile` of its size. **Icons are centred by their ink**: a symbol alone in a tile or an icon button is an `OpticalSymbol`, drawn on an even-sized square with the centre of its ink on the square's centre, so an arrow or a cup sits on the optical centre on whole pixels at 1× and 2×; `PluginIconTile`, `SymbolTile`, `IconAction` and `IconMenu` draw theirs this way. Never a bare `Image(systemName:)` alone in a tile or icon button.
- **Services have one mapping**: `ServiceMark.provider(_:)`, `.tool(_:)` and `.model(_:)` give every provider, coding tool and local model runtime its own symbol and tint, drawn as `PluginIconTile(mark:)` in Models, setup and pickers. No generic computer, terminal or server tile, and no two services alike. Status (installed, chosen, signed in) is a trailing `FrogBadge` or text, never in the tile's place.
- **Glyphs mean one thing**: `plus` adds, `stop.fill` stops (never a ring or dot, which read as record), `rectangle.center.inset.filled` centres a window, `pencil` edits, `ellipsis` opens more actions, `xmark` closes or removes, `arrow.up.forward` reveals in Finder (never a second folder beside a folder tile), `exclamationmark.triangle.fill` marks a failure in notices and the answer panel alike.
- **Rules wear the glyph their name says**: `SelectionBubbleItems.symbol(for:)` gives Explain its question bubble and any rule whose name says what it does its own symbol (Define a book, Translate a globe, an email an envelope, Proofread a checkmark, Summarize a list, Shorten scissors, Polish a wand); only a rule with no such word wears its feature's glyph. The bubble, the Writing rows and the command bar all ask it, so a rule looks the same in each.
- **Times have one format**, `RecentTimestamp`, as Mail writes them: "Just now", "12 min ago", the time today ("13:14", "09:05" with its leading zero on a 24-hour clock, so times line up), "Yesterday", the weekday within a week, then the date ("Nov 2", with the year when it is another). In `caption`, tabular digits, one line, on the row's trailing edge, the full date and time in its tooltip; a list whose times can go stale wraps them in `TimelineView(.everyMinute)`. Hover actions that need the place take it over instead of pushing the time aside.
- **A failed answer shows only the failure**: the error and its one fix (Retry, Choose model) on the status line, with no follow-up field under it. Its header always names the model as "Model · Provider".
- **The answer panel** is header (rule, model, the pin), answer, a status line only for a failure, Stopped or the limit, the follow-up field at the panel's full width, and the shared `FrogFooter`. While the answer runs, the footer's leading side breathes (`PulseDot`) beside "Thinking…" or "Answering…" and offers Stop ⌘. and Close esc; once it is done, Copy ⌘↩, Save ⌘S (with notes on) and Close esc, each turning into what it did ("Copied", "Saved"). While it streams the text ends in a thin still caret (a text cursor's weight, never a block), so the eye finds where it grows; the dot is the accent. The field stays enabled while the answer runs, so the next question can be typed and asked with Return once it is done.
- Status pills along the bottom of the screen (Working, Done, a failure, Secure Input) are `FrogNoticePill`: a capsule that hugs its text, a symbol in the tone colour, the message, and an optional muted detail after a `·` or one compact secondary button. Working says what the rule is doing, its leading verb in the -ing form ("Proofreading…", "Translating to German…"; a name that is not a command stays as it is, "Spelling only…"), with the one ellipsis character. Done names the rule and what happened, a green `checkmark.circle.fill` and "Proofread · 3 words changed" ("No changes", "Copied"); there is no Undo in the pill, because the app the text went into keeps its own (⌘Z there). A failure, behind the warning triangle, names the rule and what went wrong first ("Proofread failed: Ollama is not running"), then a Details button, set a clear gap after the message, that opens the Frog window, whose banner holds the whole message; only then does the pill take clicks, otherwise it lets them through.
- **One accent.** The theme's accent is the one colour of choosing and acting: primary buttons, chosen segments, switches that are on, ticked checkboxes, active badges, focus, graph lines, the snap-zone highlight. Minimal, the default, uses indigo. `selection` (neutral) marks the chosen row in lists and the sidebar; `hover` what the pointer is over. Green (`good`) means success or granted only, never "on" or "chosen"; `warning` and `critical` are status, never decoration. No `Color.blue`, `.green` or the system accent in a view.
- **Text has two tones**: `ink` and `muted`. Every theme's `muted` keeps at least 4.5:1 on its cards, so secondary, disabled-but-informative and hint text is `muted` at full strength, never `muted.opacity(…)` below it. `placeholder` is the one lighter step, for empty fields only.

## Motion

Frog moves little: a press answers, a hover leaves softly, a choice slides, and occasional things arrive and leave. Everything else is an instant cut.

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `press` | strong ease-out, 0.14 s | `FrogButtonStyle`: scale 0.97 and the `pressed` fill on the down event; no scale under Reduce Motion or on keyboard surfaces |
| `hoverOut` | ease 0.15 s | A hover fill leaving (`frogRowHighlight`, control surfaces, buttons). Hover in is instant |
| `slide` | ease-out (0.25, 1, 0.5, 1), 0.18 s | The thumb of `CompactSegments` and a switch's knob. It is the only thing that moves: the segments' labels are drawn once on the track and once in `onAccent` clipped to the thumb, so they never fade colour on their own and a label is light exactly where the thumb covers it. A popup whose tabs are segments (the Frog menu) swaps its content and height at once, never animated |
| `reveal` | strong ease-out (0.23, 1, 0.32, 1), 0.2 s | Occasional arrivals: the error banner, a copy confirmation, scrolling to a section |
| `exit` | strong ease-out, 0.15 s | The same leaving, faster than it came |
| `check` | strong ease-out, 0.15 s | `FrogCheckbox`: the mark grows in from 0.8 as the box fills |
| `step` | spring, response 0.35, no bounce | Steps of the setup assistant: in 16 points from the side they lie on |
| `fadeOut` | strong ease-out, 0.2 s | Status pills and status item popups fading out (`NSWindow.fadeOut()`) |
| `resize` | ease-in-out, 0.22 s | The dictation pill growing with its text; it changes shape on screen |
| `pulse` | ease-in-out, 0.9 s, repeating | The answer panel's dot breathing while it waits; still under Reduce Motion |
| `confirmation` | 1.5 s | How long `CopyAction` shows its checkmark |

- Never animate the command bar, clipboard history, window switcher or layout picker: opening, closing, typing, arrow keys. Their roots set `.environment(\.frogKeyboardSurface, true)`, which turns off hover fades and press motion inside them.
- Never animate selection moved by arrow keys or Tab (dropdown lists set `frogKeyboardSurface` for this), page switches (sidebar and ⌘1–5), or text that streams in.
- No bounce anywhere: nothing in Frog is thrown or flicked. No ease-in: it starts slow where the eye is watching.
- Reduce Motion: no scale and no sliding; segments, setup steps, the error banner and the checkbox mark cross-fade instead.
- Copy actions use `CopyAction`: its symbol turns into a checkmark for `confirmation`.
- An animation names a token from `Motion.swift`: `withAnimation(FrogMotion.reveal)`, never `.easeOut(duration:)`, a bare `withAnimation` or a literal `NSAnimationContext` duration. `StyleTokenScanTests` checks it.

## Charts and meters

Every graph and meter is built from `FrogApp/Views/Design/FrogChart.swift`, so System monitor and Usage look like one family.

- Lines are smooth (`.monotone`, never overshooting), 2 points with round caps and joins (`FrogChart.line`). A second series is 1.5 points and uses `FrogStyle.seriesSecondary`, a teal that stands apart from the accent (indigo beside a green or teal accent), never a grey that reads as disabled; the main series uses `FrogStyle.series` (the accent). Warning and critical colours colour meters only, through `FrogStyle.level`. A usage meter is the accent while it is on or behind pace, `warning` when it is ahead of pace and `critical` from 95 %; its pace text is muted unless it is ahead or the limit is reached. Never green for "under pace".
- The main line sits on a fill that fades from 35 % of its colour to clear (`FrogChart.areaFill`). Daily amounts are thin rounded `BarMark`s in the accent at low opacity.
- Minimal chrome: no axes, a few faint dashed grid lines in `hairline` (`FrogChartGrid`), and short muted labels only where they help: 0, 50 and 100 % left of the wide CPU graph (`FrogChartScaleGutter`, `FrogChart.scaleGutter` wide, the time axis indented to match), the two end dates or times under a graph (`GraphAxis`). A graph with two kinds of mark names both in a key under it. The plot is clipped to a nested corner (`FrogChart.plotRadius`: `controlRadius` on pages, `smallRadius` in popups).
- **LLM usage's page chart** is daily (or hourly) rounded bars in the accent on one baseline, with a leading y-scale in `FrogChartScaleGutter` at 0, half and a round top figure (1, 2, 2.5 or 5 times a power of ten). It draws no running-total line: the figure above states the total and the pointer chip gives the running total. A day with a little activity keeps a short stub; a day with none has no bar. A popup's small chart is the same bars, 56 tall, the first and last bar on the plot's edges, its two end dates under it and a chip on hover; it chooses its range with `CompactSegments`, not a dropdown. The y-scale's zero is written in the scale's own unit ("$0", never a bare "0").
- The newest value of the main series ends in a filled dot with a soft halo (`FrogChartPoint`). Under the pointer: a thin rule (`FrogChartRule`), a dot on each line and a rounded chip (`FrogChartChip`) with the values and the time. The chip takes the corner away from the pointer, so it stays inside the plot.
- System monitor shows one window, `SystemMonitor.historyWindow` (10 minutes), in its subtitle, its axis ("10 min ago" to "Now") and its popup. The graph's card holds only what it draws: CPU and GPU, each a legend dot, its reading and a `FrogMeter`. Memory and storage share the card below. Memory is what is used of the total ("10.62 GB of 16 GB") with its meter filled to the same share. Storage says what is free and its share ("182 GB free · 37% of 494 GB"), its meter filled with that same free share, so the bar and the headline name one quantity (`warning` under 15 % free, `critical` under 5 %), and under it the change against about 24 hours ago ("−3.4 GB since yesterday", with a true minus; "Same as yesterday" within 50 MB). The sampler keeps a free-space reading every half hour for two days in Frog's data (`SystemMonitorStorageLog`, `system-monitor-storage.json`), so the change survives a restart; before it has a reading from 20 to 28 hours ago the line says the change shows after a day. On the page each card has its heading (Load, Memory and storage, Power), and every two-column row in a card (CPU and GPU, memory and storage, the battery's figures) has equal columns one `columnGap` (32 on the page, 16 in the popup) apart, so second columns share one edge. The loading state draws the same meters' empty tracks under its skeletons, each skeleton bar on a line exactly as tall as the text that replaces it (`SkeletonText`), so the loading popup is the loaded one's height. A legend dot means a line of that colour on the graph.
- Before there is a line, `FrogChartPlaceholder` shows a dashed baseline and a calm note at the graph's own height, so nothing moves when data arrives; where the figures' skeleton bars already say it is measuring (System monitor), it shows a faint skeleton line in `FrogStyle.skeleton` instead of a note. The CPU and GPU meters take their line's colour (accent, teal) until they run high, then their level's, so GPU never reads as off.
- `FrogMeter` is the one horizontal meter: a capsule track in `FrogStyle.track`, a fill that fades from lighter to full level colour with a soft highlight, and an optional crisp tick (the share of time gone in a plan window). It has one height, `FrogMeter.height` (6), on pages and in popups; the tick is an overlay on whole points, so it never changes the meter's size. The fill glides with `FrogMotion.reveal` and does not move under Reduce Motion.
- Nothing animates continuously. Graphs are drawn from bounded data (`SystemMonitorGraph.maxPoints`) and are `Equatable`, so an unrelated update does not redraw them.
