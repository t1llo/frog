# Design rules

Frog looks quiet and consistent: Raycast's dense, keyboard-first popups, and a settings window in the manner of macOS System Settings and the Rex terminal: a sidebar panel floating inside the window with back and forward beside the window's buttons, the page's title in a toolbar on the page's content edge, and borderless cards whose rows are set apart by space. Every value comes from [DesignSystem.swift](../native/FrogApp/Views/DesignSystem.swift), [CompactControls.swift](../native/FrogApp/Views/CompactControls.swift), [Motion.swift](../native/FrogApp/Views/Motion.swift) and the v2 components in [Views/Design](../native/FrogApp/Views/Design). A view never invents its own button, dropdown, radius, height, text size, material or animation. `StyleTokenScanTests` checks the rules that code can check.

[DesignGallery](../native/FrogApp/Views/Design/DesignGallery.swift) shows every component. After changing one, draw it and look: `FROG_PAGE_SHOTS=<folder> swift test --filter 'FrogAppTests.PageShotsTests/testDesignGallery'` writes `design-gallery-light.png` and `design-gallery-dark.png`.

## Less text

- A page has a title and at most one line of subtitle. A section has a caption and at most one sentence of footer.
- A row has a title (a noun: "Shortcut", "Model") and at most one short caption. Longer explanations go in `.help()`.
- Say a thing once: not in the subtitle, the caption and a footer.
- **One warning per problem.** A page or sheet says what is wrong once. Where a `ProblemNotice` (or `ModelMissingNotice`) says it, the control that fixes it carries no second mark: its title asks ("Choose a model"), and the notice's button opens it (`RuleModelPicker(marksMissing: false)`). Without a notice, the control is marked once: `FrogDropdown`'s `.warning("Reason")` swaps its symbol for the warning triangle, a row the dropdown is not in gets one `ProblemMark`, or the row's caption says why in the warning colour. Never a triangle beside the control and a caption under it as well.
- Name things the same everywhere: "Previous display", "Add rule", and coding tools "use their own sign-in" (not "login").
- **Full stops follow the grammar, not the slot.** A caption, subtitle, footer or message that is a sentence ends with a full stop: it has a verb of its own, a statement ("Frog installs it when it quits.") or an instruction ("Hold this shortcut to see every rule and its keys."). A fragment does not: a noun phrase, a list or a label, even with a clause inside it ("Shell scripts that run with zsh in your home folder", "Up to 200 MB each", "1.6 GB · WhisperKit"). Text of more than one part ends every part with one ("Kept in memory. Passwords and keys never are."). Titles, buttons, badges, menu items and tooltips that name an action never have one.
  - A feature's or plugin's summary (`PluginInfo.summary`, a directory entry) is a tagline: a short phrase without a full stop, even when it starts with a verb ("Speak instead of typing"), in Features, the command bar and setup alike. A command bar row's subtitle is a label in the same way ("Shows the answer in a panel"). A summary from outside Frog loses its trailing full stop where it joins a `·` caption.

## No lines in the chrome

- Cards are not split into rows by lines: `FrogSection` sets its rows apart by their height (`rowHeight`) and a little space, so every card on every page looks the same whether its rows are `FrogRow`s or a page's own.
- No `Divider()`, no `NSMenuItem.separator()`, and no thin `Rectangle` above footers, beside sidebars or anywhere else.
- Separate everything with space, a heading, or a surface: a card, an inset fill, or the footer band.
- A popup's footer sits on `frogFooterBand()` (`FrogStyle.footerBand`, a shade darker than the panel), never under a line: `PanelFooter`, `FrogPopupFooter` and any footer a popup draws itself.
- Exceptions: the user's own content (Markdown rules and quote bars), chart axes and gridlines, progress tracks, and the drop indicator while dragging.

## Radii

Corners nest. A shape inside another has the outer radius minus the gap between them, so both curves share one centre and the gap stays even all the way round. Every row, field and highlight is `controlRadius`; each container is that plus the gap its content keeps from its edge, and the tokens are defined that way in `FrogStyle`, so they cannot drift apart.

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `smallRadius` | 4 | Key caps, code chips, colour swatches: marks inside a control |
| `controlRadius` | 10 | Fields, rows and their highlight, menu rows, symbol tiles, blocks inside a card or popup, a popup's search field |
| `cardRadius` | 14 = 10 + `listInset` 4 | Cards, settings groups, lists, previews, banners; a list's rows sit 4 in |
| `panelRadius` | 18 = 10 + `panelInset` 8 | Popups and floating panels (`frogPanel()`); their search field, rows and blocks sit 8 in, and a card inside one sits `panelCardInset` (4) in |
| `windowRadius` | 28 on macOS 26 | The main window's own corner, drawn by macOS (measured) |
| `sidebarRadius` | 20 = 28 - `sidebarInset` 8 | The main window's sidebar panel; its rows sit `sidebarRowInset` (10) in |
| `pillRadius` | 18 | 36-point status pills (`frogPanel(pill: true)`), drawn as true capsules: a continuous corner of half the height flattens the round ends |

Buttons, search boxes, segmented controls and switches are capsules, as in Rex; so are badges (`FrogBadge`), status pills, dots, avatars, progress bars and skeleton bars. No other numbers. Cards, controls and the sidebar panel use the `.continuous` corner style.

## Sizes and spacing

- Controls are `controlHeight` (28) tall; in popups, panel headers and dense rows `compactHeight` (24).
- **Two action heights.** Every action beside a row's text (a button, an icon action, a recorder's "Record shortcut") is 24: `FrogRow` marks its control `frogRowAccessory()`, which makes `FrogButtonStyle`, `IconAction`, `IconMenu` and the add buttons compact; a page that draws its own rows puts `.frogRowAccessory()` on their trailing controls. Page header and sheet actions are 28. Choices (`FrogDropdown`, `CompactSegments`) keep their own height.
- Pages: `FrogPage` (padding `pagePadding` 20, sections `sectionSpacing` 30 apart, a column at most `pageWidth` 720 wide; `FrogStyle.wideWidth` 1080 for dashboards and tables) with a `FrogPageHeader` (title, optional one-line subtitle, actions on the trailing side). The column starts `pagePadding` from the leading edge on every page, whatever its width: a wide page only reaches further to the trailing side. A page never adds its own horizontal inset.
- **One page header.** Every page and step inside a page has one `FrogPageHeader`: the title, one line of subtitle under it, and the actions on the trailing side, level with them. In the main window all three go up to the toolbar, as a Mac window shows a title and subtitle: the title (`pageTitle`) and the subtitle (`small`, muted) stacked level with the window's buttons, the actions (28 tall, `FrogAddButton` among them when the page adds things) ending at the column's trailing edge. The header then takes no room in the page (`FrogPageStack` gives it no spacing), so the first section starts right under the toolbar. Never replace the subtitle with chips or an empty row: filters go in the page, under the toolbar. A sheet draws the same header itself, its title in `title`. A sub-page (History's entry, the plugin Directory, Models' tabs) shows a breadcrumb in the same `pageTitle` size, the parent muted.
- **Tabbed pages** (Models, Community plugins, History's filters): the tabs are `CompactSegments` leading the first row under the toolbar, in a `ListToolbar` when the tab has a search. The page keeps one subtitle for every tab; what a single tab needs to say goes inside that tab (a notice, a section footer). The toolbar shows at most the one add action of the tab that is open.
- **Two edges in the main window.** The toolbar's title and subtitle, a page's filters and search, and its cards start on the column's leading edge. Everything that reads as text of a section starts `rowInset` (14, the card padding) in: the section's heading, the rows' text inside the card and the footer under it, so a section reads down one line, as in System Settings. Nothing else is a third edge.
- The main window: an empty `NSToolbar` makes the title bar 52 points tall, so the window's buttons sit inside the sidebar panel, as in Rex. The sidebar panel is 208 wide, floating `sidebarInset` (8) inside the window with `sidebarRadius`; back and forward are two plain chevrons on the trailing side of its top strip, level with the window's buttons (⌘[ and ⌘]). Beside it a toolbar `toolbarHeight` (52) tall holds the open page's header. `FrogPageHeader` hands its title (`FrogPageTitleKey`), subtitle (`FrogPageSubtitleKey`) and actions (`FrogPageActionsKey`) up to it; `FrogPage` hands up its width. A sheet is its own window, so its header sets `.environment(\.frogToolbarTitle, false)` and draws its title.
- A section is `FrogSection("Heading", footer:)`: a heading (`FrogSectionHeader`), one card, an optional one-sentence footer. Inside it every setting is a `FrogRow("Title", caption:) { control }`, at least `rowHeight` (40: a 28-point control and `space6` above and below, so dropdown, segment and switch rows are one height) tall, its title in `row` (13), its text `rowInset` (14) from the card's edge. Rows are set apart by space, not lines.
- **Headings.** Every group on a page or in a sheet has the one heading style, `FrogSectionHeader` (`section` 13 semibold, the text colour, `rowInset` in, trailing actions in `small` muted ending where the rows' controls end). A page that groups its own content (a list, a grid) puts a `FrogSectionHeader` above it, the first card included. Give every card group on a page a heading, or none of them. **A heading never repeats the page's or tab's name**: a tab that holds one list ("Installed" under Community plugins) shows its card with no heading, and a page's only section has none ("Dictation" on Dictation). **One counter form**: a count belongs to its heading as `FrogSectionHeader("Apps", count: 23)` (`FrogSection(count:)`), the number muted in tabular `row` digits after the title, read as "Apps, 23"; never "Apps · 23", "(23)", "23 apps" or "4 of 23" in the title. `SectionCaption` follows where it is: inside a card or a popup it is the small muted caption of a sub-group; outside one it draws as the section heading, at the same inset. Never add your own padding to a heading.
- **A step inside a page** (a History entry, Statistics inside Settings, an app under review in App cleaner, a community plugin's page under Community plugins) is `.frogPageBack(id, parent: "History") { … }`: the toolbar shows a breadcrumb, "History › Proofread", whose first part goes back, and Back and ⌘[ take the step before the previous page. That is the only back pattern: a page draws no "‹ History" link of its own and never binds ⌘[. A sentence that belongs to no row is a `FrogRowNote`; other content (previews, button rows) goes in a `VStack(spacing: 10).padding(.vertical, 10)`.
- **The one row recipe.** Every row in a card starts its icon or text `rowInset` (14) in from the card's edge and ends its trailing control `rowInset` in, so icons, titles and switches line up down the window whatever page they are on. There are two ways to get there and no third: a `FrogRow` inside a `FrogSection` (the section supplies the inset), or a list whose rows highlight on hover, which is a card padded `listInset` (4) holding rows padded `rowPadding` (10) on both sides (4 + 10 = 14). A page never pads a row with its own number. Menus follow the same split inside their cards: `MenuActionRow` and `StayAwakeMenuRow` pad `rowPadding` inside a `listInset` card, so the switch ends where the keycaps do.
- Cards in a popup or a menu tab are `panelCardGap` (4, the same as their `panelCardInset` from the panel) apart in every menu (Frog, System, Usage, the LLM usage and System monitor popups). Every tab puts its short status on the footer's leading side (`MenuContribution.status`: System's "Live", Usage's "16m ago"), and its header row leads with its controls.
- `FrogRow(…, icon: { PluginIconTile(…) })` puts a feature's icon before the title.
- A caption that is a file path is `FrogRow("Model folder", caption: .path(url.path))`: one line, home as "~", cut in the middle, the full path on hover. A row about a file (a failure in App cleaner) is `FrogRow(path:caption:)`, the path as its title the same way. A caption that names a command is `caption: .attributed(InlineCode.attributed("Run `frog plugin publish` …"))`. No page rebuilds `FrogRow`'s layout by hand for these.
- v1 names, for pages not moved yet: `PageScroll` and `PageHeader` now draw `FrogPage` (wide) and `FrogPageHeader`; `SettingsSection` with `CompactRow` (card padding 14, rows 10 apart) still works. Move a page to `FrogSection` and `FrogRow` all at once, never one section of it.
- Inside a card, a sub-group starts with a `SectionCaption`.
- The sidebar always shows the open page's row: Models, Features and Settings are pinned under the scrolling list, and opening a plugin's page from anywhere scrolls the list by the least amount that brings its row into view (Statistics shows Settings).
- List rows are 1–2 apart, highlighted with `frogRowHighlight(hovering:selected:)`, or `frogHoverRow(selected:)` when the row tracks its own hover.
- Spacing comes from the `FrogStyle.space2`…`space16` scale (2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16), never an odd value such as 7 or 9: 2 between a title and its caption, 4 inside a label, 6–8 between controls in a row, 10 between blocks inside a card, 12 between a row's icon, text and control, 16 between groups in a popup.
- One content edge per surface, so text lines up from the header to the footer:
  - Cards, section cards, popup headers, popup bodies and `PanelFooter` start content `cardPadding` (14) from the edge.
  - A list is inset `listInset` (4) and its rows padded `rowPadding` (10): 4 + 10 = 14, the same edge. A search or query line above the rows gives its icon the rows' icon width and gap, so the query starts where the titles do.
  - Sheets and setup use `sheetPadding` (20) on every side, header, body and buttons alike; the onboarding window uses `pagePadding` (20) like a page.
  - A ghost button that starts a line (a back link, Skip setup) puts its text on the content edge with `.padding(.leading, -10)`; only its hover fill reaches past it.
  - A ghost button in a section header's accessory (Refresh, Clear, Select all) ends its text on the trailing edge by itself: `FrogSectionHeader` sets `frogTrailingGhost`, and the button pulls its own padding past the edge. Call sites add no negative padding.
- In a sheet, `FrogPageHeader`'s title is a control tall, so it sits at the same height whatever the subtitle or actions; actions are 8 apart. Icon actions in a page header are plain (`IconAction`, `IconMenu(bordered: false)`).
- The sidebar: the window's buttons, a `SearchBox` that narrows the rows, Frog's own row (a 32-point `FrogMark`, "Frog" and a status line; it opens Settings with ⌘, and says which permission setup still needs), then the plugin groups and Community pages, which scroll, and Models, Features and Settings pinned at the bottom. Each plugin group is headed by its name (`SidebarGroupHeader`: `label` semibold, muted, `sidebarHeaderHeight` 18, 6 in like the rows' tiles, as Finder's sidebar heads Favorites and Locations), and set apart by one `sidebarGroupGap` (10); Frog's row and the pinned Models, Features and Settings have no header. Community plugins and the pages plugins add share one group, **Plugins**, every plugin page with the community tint, so nothing a plugin adds floats unheaded at the list's end. Headers are rows of the grid, so the scroll rests on them like on any row, and the last row is never cut; at the default window size (1000 × 760, `MainView.defaultSize`) the built-in rows fit without scrolling. When they do not (Phone, community plugins, a smaller window), Models, Features and Settings stop being pinned and scroll at the list's end, as Finder's and Mail's sidebars scroll as one list, and the list fades out over the panel's bottom edge. The list's scroll rests only on a row's top edge (`SidebarRowGrid`, `SidebarRowSnap`), and revealing the open row scrolls on to the next row edge, so a row never shows cut in half under the search field or above the pinned rows, at any window height; while it moves, rows pass under a crisp edge one group gap below the field. Rows are `sidebarRowHeight` (28) tall with a `PluginIconTile(.sidebar)` and the title in `row`; the open row is filled with the neutral `sidebarSelection`, never the accent.

## Type

Use `FrogType` for text. A literal size is only for an SF Symbol's size. The scale is three clear steps: a page title (20) above a sheet title (17) above a section heading (13 semibold), so a page never reads as a sheet and a section never as a page.

| Token | Use |
| --- | --- |
| `hero` 26 bold | The welcome headline in setup, the one hero line |
| `pageTitle` 20 bold | The open page's title in the main window's toolbar, and every sub-page's breadcrumb, at the same size |
| `title` 17 semibold | Sheet titles, setup step titles, standalone panels (shortcut reference, window cheat sheet), big figures |
| `row` 13 | Row titles in section cards, sidebar rows |
| `rowMedium` 13 semibold | The sidebar's Frog row |
| `section` 13 semibold | Section headings, popup titles, empty-state titles |
| `lead` 13 | Sheet subtitles, empty-state messages |
| `body` 13 | List items, field text, dropdown values, menu rows |
| `bodyMedium` 12 medium | Buttons, titles of cards and settings |
| `small` 11 | Row descriptions, secondary lines, compact controls, a page's subtitle in the toolbar |
| `label` 11 medium | `SectionCaption`, compact buttons, key caps |
| `caption` 10 | Timestamps, paths, counts, badges, footnotes |
| `mono` 11 | Code, file contents and identifiers only. Not shortcuts |

## Buttons

`FrogButtonStyle` is the only button. The main window sets the secondary kind for every button in it.

- `.buttonStyle(.frog(.primary))`: the one main action of a page, sheet or card, filled with the accent and lettered in `onAccent` (white, or the theme's dark surface on a pastel accent).
- Secondary (default): every other action.
- `.frog(.destructive)`: removes or deletes something, a grey pill lettered in `critical`.
- **Three looks, no fourth**: primary, secondary and destructive are the whole set in the window, sheets, popups and rows. A cancel, a Clear, a Rename or a Refresh in a header is a secondary pill; a remove that is one of many per row is an `IconAction` (`xmark`).
- `.frog(.ghost)` is kept only for the setup assistant's way-finding links (Back, Skip, Skip setup, Import settings…): the label in `ink` with no fill until hovered. Nowhere else; `StyleTokenScanTests` keeps it there.
- **Disabled**: one look for every kind, the secondary pill with its fill at `disabledOpacity` (0.6) and its label in `muted`, so it still says what it would do; a disabled primary drops its accent, a disabled ghost stays plain muted text. **Live or disabled is told by the label's tone**: an enabled button's label is always `ink` (or `onAccent`, `critical`), a disabled one's always `muted`. So a ghost is never muted at rest, or Skip beside a disabled Continue reads as two dead buttons. Never fade a label further. A disabled primary action says why with `.frogDisabled("Choose a model first")` (tooltip and VoiceOver hint) instead of `.disabled(true)`; when the reason fits on the page, show it as a row caption too. Switches, checkboxes and dropdowns fade by the same `disabledOpacity`.
- `compact: true` in popups, panel headers and notices; rows get it from `frogRowAccessory`. A button that goes with a field (Review beside the link field) is `controlHeight` like the field, with `frogRowAccessory(false)`.
- **Adding something** is `FrogAddButton("Add rule")`: a secondary pill with a plus, in the page header (or a section header for a section's own list). When the user first picks what to add ("Add app"), the same pill with a chevron (`FrogAddLabel(menu: true)`), which only a list has, opening a searchable `FrogDropdownList`, as Shortcuts' add buttons do. Never a bare "+ New", a popup-menu text button or an icon alone.
- **Sheet buttons** are `FrogSheetButtons { destructive } trailing: { FrogCancelButton { }; FrogPrimaryButton("Save", disabledReason:) { } }`: Delete (or Remove) alone on the leading side, Cancel next to the primary on the trailing side, the primary last. Escape presses Cancel, Return the primary. Back in a multi-step sheet goes between Cancel and the primary. `FrogSheetButtons(padded: false)` drops its own `sheetPadding` when the sheet already pads its content, as the small Rename and Try sheets do.
- `IconAction` for icon-only actions: a plain icon that highlights on hover. `bordered: true` only in a popup header, beside compact segments, and in a menu-bar menu tab's header row, which is that popup's header in the menu (refresh and open in Usage are boxed in both).
- `IconMenu` for a menu behind an icon ("…" by default): drawn exactly like the `IconAction` beside it. Never `.menuStyle(.borderlessButton)`, which drops Frog's surface and hover.
- `.buttonStyle(.plain)` only for rows, tiles and cards that draw their own hover with `frogRowHighlight`.
- Never `.bordered`, `.borderedProminent`, `.borderless` or `.link`.

## Controls

- **Every single choice is a `FrogDropdown`.** Never `Picker`, a `Menu` used as a picker, `CompactMenu` or a hand-made popover list. It is generic over a `Hashable` value: `FrogDropdown(selection: $value, options: values, title: { $0.title }, symbol: { … })`, or `selected:`/`choose:` when the choice is saved through an action. For an optional choice make the value optional and offer `nil` ("System default", "No model").
  - Sections and rich rows: `sections: [FrogDropdownSection("Downloaded", values: …)]` with `item: { FrogDropdownItem(title, detail:, symbol:, badge:, disabled:) }`.
  - Long lists (models, apps, microphones, languages; anything over about 8): `searchPrompt: "Find a model"` turns on the search field on the same list. Search matches title and detail.
  - `compact: true` in popups and panel headers; `width:` for the button (220 by default); `.buttonTitle { … }` when the button should say less than the row; `.disabled(…)` fades it by `disabledOpacity`; `.warning(reason)` marks it as the control that fixes a problem (the triangle in `warning`, the reason as its tooltip).
  - The list opens on the chosen row. Arrow keys move, Return chooses, Escape closes; the highlight never animates.
- Choices of two to four, and switching between parts of a page (Providers, Installed tools): `CompactSegments`, a capsule whose chosen segment is filled with the accent. Segments are as wide as their labels.
- Choices that are best seen, such as the colour scheme, Classic or Glass, and the theme: tiles with a picture and the name under it, the chosen one ringed in the accent (Appearance settings).
- `IconMenu` is for actions behind an icon (Rename, Delete, Add an app), not for choosing a value.
- On/off settings: `Toggle` with `.toggleStyle(.frogSwitch)`: a 36 by 20 capsule with a round white knob 2 in. On fills it with `switchOn`, the accent; off is the neutral `switchTrack`. While the window is not in front, and while it is disabled, an on switch is a darker grey than off (never a pale wash of the accent), and a disabled switch fades. A setting that can still be changed is never dimmed: dim only what is `.disabled`. `FrogRow` applies it to its control, and the row's title names it.
- Picking items from a list (files to remove, models to offer, agents to set up): `.toggleStyle(.frogCheckbox)`. Never the system checkbox.
- Numbers from a short list: `FrogDropdown` rather than a `Stepper`.
- Text: `LabeledField` or `frogControlSurface()`, with a `FrogTextField(placeholder:text:)` inside: it draws the placeholder in `FrogStyle.placeholder`, a step lighter than muted, where a bare `TextField` draws it nearly as dark as a value. No `.roundedBorder`.
- **Search has one place**: the row directly above the list it filters, as a `ListToolbar`: filters or tabs leading, the `SearchBox` trailing at `ListToolbar.searchWidth` (220); with no filters it fills the row. Never in the page header, the toolbar or beside an add button. In a list-and-editor page (Notes, Scripts) it heads the list column. `showsSearch: false` hides the field while the tabs keep their place. The sidebar and dropdown lists keep their own field. Filters that narrow a list (All, Text, Audio): `FilterTag`.
- **Shortcuts are caps, everywhere**: `KeyCaps("⌃⌥Space")` or `KeyCaps(hotkey:)`, one 18-point `KeyCap` per key, in the sans font: in rows, sheets, footers, menus, lists, layout tiles and the shortcut reference. A recorded shortcut that can be changed is a `HotkeyRecorder`: its caps on a faint hover, clicked to record again. One that can be cleared keeps a clear button (`xmark.circle.fill`) beside its caps, shown only while the pointer is over the recorder or it records, and offered to VoiceOver as an action; a required shortcut (Command bar, Clipboard history, Window switcher, the Shortcuts list) passes `showsClearButton: false`. Only while recording (or before anything is recorded) does it draw a pill ("Press shortcut…", "Record shortcut"). `ShortcutBadge` is the same caps for a shortcut shown where it cannot be changed. Never glue a shortcut's glyphs into one chip or write it as plain text: `KeyCaps(["⌥", "N"])` for a list of keys, and `KeyHintLine(text:)` for a hint written as text ("⌥Space to stop · Esc to cancel"), which draws each shortcut as caps. Bare `KeyCap(` is only for one key. Where a list or grid has a slot for a shortcut that is not set, the slot stays empty and its recorder appears on hover or keyboard focus; the section header counts how many are set. Never a dash or a footnote explaining the empty slot. The shortcut reference groups by feature, named as the sidebar names it: Writing, Explain, Dictation, Windows (window layouts and their rules), then Frog. A group that would hold one row naming its own page (a lone Explain under Explain) joins Frog's own shortcuts instead. Window layout tiles in the picker reserve the keycap slot on every tile, set or not, so all tiles are one height and their labels line up.
- Waiting: `FrogSpinner` is the one spinner (not a bare `ProgressView()`): eight spokes in `ink` fading round, Frog's own drawing because the system's indicator was too faint on dark panels; `FrogLoadingNote(text: "Looking for apps…")` is a spinner and one muted line inside a card; values still being measured are `FrogSkeleton` bars of about the value's width, so nothing moves when they arrive. Say "Measuring…" once for a group at most, never per value. A download or removal with a known share done is a determinate `ProgressView(value:)`. Skeleton bars use `FrogStyle.skeleton`, a step above the control fill so they show on a light card. Accent words on a card or `accentSoft` ("Recommended", an accent `FrogBadge`) use `FrogStyle.accentText`, the accent eased until it reads at 4.5:1. A chart's scale labels stand in a column left of the plot (`FrogChartScaleGutter`), each centred on its gridline, never on the plot.
- **Empty lists and pages: one recipe**, `FrogEmptyState`: a 40-point tile, a `section` title, one `lead` sentence and the action that fills it, unless the page header already has that action, 32 points of space above and below, in its card. Whatever belongs to a feature (its page, its list, a feature that is off) passes the feature's `tint:` so the tile is the feature's coloured tile with a white symbol; only what belongs to no feature ("No matches", a load that failed) keeps the neutral `SymbolTile`. Never a bare glyph, a lone grey line in a card, or a card taller than its content.
- **Warnings: once per page, with the fix.** A problem that keeps a page's feature from working is one `ProblemNotice` above its sections, always with the button that fixes it (`ModelMissingNotice` opens the flagged model picker via `modelPickerOpenRequest`, or Models when there is nothing to choose). The control at fault is marked once, inside it (`FrogDropdown(warning:)`). Rows get their own mark only to single out some of them, never when every row is affected. A sheet never repeats in its footer what a control in it already marks.

## Surfaces and colour

- Cards: `FrogCard`, or `frogTableSurface()` for lists that draw their own rows. A card is a thin layer of the text colour over the page (`FrogStyle.card`, 3.5 % light and 7.5 % dark) with no border, so it lifts off the page in every theme, dark ones included; controls are a step above (`control`, 6 % / 12 %), meter and progress tracks use `track` (8 % / 13 %) so they show on a card, Increase Contrast adds an outline. Blocks inside a card use the inset fill with `controlRadius` and no border.
- Controls (buttons, fields, search boxes) are filled with `FrogStyle.control`, one step above a card, and have no outline unless Increase Contrast is on. A dropdown is Rex's: the value in plain text and a small round button with an up-and-down chevron. In a row its knob, like a recorder's last cap, ends on the content edge level with switches and buttons; only the hover fill reaches past. `.fitToContent()` makes the button only as wide as its value, so a label sits right against it (Scripts' Time limit); never `.fixedSize()` around a dropdown.
- Popups and floating panels are the window's own canvas colour with a hairline edge; their footer sits on the footer band, a shade darker, with no line.
- **One footer**, `FrogFooter { status } actions: { FrogFooterHint("Paste", keys: "↩") }`, for every popup, panel and menu: the status on the left (muted `small`, one line, it keeps its room before the actions do), the actions on the right as ink words each followed by its caps, on `frogFooterBand()`. The status is laid out first; where the row is too narrow for both, each hint drops its word and keeps its caps (the word stays in its tooltip), and only a status longer than the whole row truncates. The Notes and Scripts editor card ends in a plain row on the card's own fill instead (when it was edited, then the item's own controls ending on the content edge), as its bar along the top is; its title heads its text, renamed in place. Status that belongs to the open content ("Updated 3 min ago", "Live", a count) lives here; a menu tab hands its status up through `MenuContribution.status`, short enough that Settings and Quit keep their words: Frog's "Ready", System's "Live", Usage's "16m ago" (the oldest reading, every account in the tooltip). **Every launcher and panel footer reads the same way**: on the left the surface's icon and name, a count muted after a `·` ("Clipboard · 6 items", "Window switcher · 23 windows"); on the right the primary action with ↩, Actions ⌘K where there are actions, and Close esc (Back esc inside a command's argument), in that order. Cards end 8 above the band (`panelCardInset` on the other sides), in popups and in Frog's menu alike. `PanelFooter` (keyboard panels) and `FrogPopupFooter` (status item popups) are this footer with fixed actions.
- Wool, Pewter, Deep Sea, Inkwell, Polar Night and Eggshell follow palettes of the Rex terminal: the page darkest, the sidebar panel a step above it, cards a step above that.
- Popups: `frogPanel()`, rows `MenuActionRow` or `MenuRowStyle`, footer `PanelFooter`.
- A scroll view whose content runs on below its bottom edge fades that edge with `bottomScrollFade()`, so a cut-off row reads as more to come. The fade is the hint: no "More below" button or pill is laid over the edge. One that scrolls under something pinned above it fades its top edge with `topScrollFade()` as well; the sidebar instead rests only on row edges, so nothing is cut under its search field. Every sheet with a scrolling body wraps it in `FrogSheetScroll(maxHeight:)`: as tall as its content up to the limit, then it scrolls and fades `FrogSheetScrollMetrics.fade` (32) above the sheet's buttons, so no footer line or row is cut in half there. Content that fits shows no fade and no scroll bar. Never a fixed `frame(height:)` around a sheet's body. No view draws its own fade mask.
- **Shortcut panels** (the shortcut reference, the window cheat sheet) are one card under the panel's title: each group a `SectionCaption` inside it, each shortcut a 30-point row (`menuRowHeight`) with its glyph, title and caps, through the shared `ShortcutPanelLayout`, `ShortcutPanelCard`, `ShortcutPanelCaption` and `ShortcutPanelRow`. No one-row cards, no subtitles on single rows. A key cap that shows one glyph is exactly square, so ⌃⌥↩ lines up with ⌃⌥→.
- A plugin's popup is the template too: the web page draws its card with Frog's card fill (`--frog-card` on a `body::before` layer, so the canvas stays clear and no band shows under it) and `PluginPopupShell`'s footer names it "Community plugin".
- **The status item popup template** (System, LLM usage and every popup under a menu-bar item): `FrogPopupHeader(symbol:tint:title:)` on top, the feature's `PluginIconTile` and name in `section`, its actions (compact segments, `IconAction(bordered: true)`) on the trailing side, 40 tall; then the body's cards `panelCardInset` in; then `FrogPopupFooter { status }`, the one footer with Close `esc` on the right. A keyboard panel (command bar, clipboard history, window switcher, layout picker) uses `PanelFooter` instead, which names the panel and what Return and ⌘K do.
- **Command bar rows** each have a tile and a muted kind on the trailing side, from `CommandRowKind`: Page (Frog's own pages), Command, Application, Window layout, Clipboard, Note and so on; no row goes without one. A feature's command is named by what it does ("Show window list"), never by the page's name, so it never looks like the page beside it.
- A rule's row in the command bar wears its owner feature's tint and says that feature as its subtitle (Writing, Explain, Dictation); a window layout says "Windows". Window rules are not listed as rules, because each is already a layout row: one thing, one row. With nothing typed the bar suggests a mix, never one kind: two rules, a command, a layout, then pages, five in all (`CommandBar.starterSuggestions`).
- **Keyboard panels search on a borderless line**: the command bar, clipboard history and the layout picker draw no field box, only a 15-point magnifier in the rows' icon column and the query in `FrogType.query` (15), a step above the rows, as Raycast does. Arguments stay chips on the control fill.
- The dictation popup's footer is a `FrogFooter`: where the words go ("Stop in Notes") on the leading side, then Stop (or "Release to stop" while a held key records) and Cancel `esc` as `FrogFooterHint`s. It shows no second Stop button or hint line.
- Badges: `FrogBadge(text: "Recommended")`, a caption in an 18-point capsule, accent-tinted when `active`; `tone: .warning` with a small symbol for a caution (Caution beside a plugin permission that reaches the user's data). A page's on/off state beside its header is `FrogStatePill`: while on, a green dot and the word in a faint green capsule; while off, a hollow muted dot and the muted word with no fill, so it never reads as a button. A command to type is a `FrogCodeChip` (mono, copies on click); commands inside a sentence are `InlineCode.attributed("Run `frog plugin new`")`.
- Backgrounds: `frogSurface(.window | .sidebar | .popover | .hud)`, the one place that picks a material. Never `NSVisualEffectView`, `.background(.ultraThinMaterial)` or `glassEffect` in a view.
  - Opaque (transparency 0, the default, or Reduce Transparency / Increase Contrast): a plain fill, nothing blurs.
  - Translucent: the kind's `NSVisualEffectView` blur under the theme's colour, the same strength for every kind, so one setting looks the same on the window and every popup. A floating panel adds a light sheen at its top and a brighter top edge (`glassSheen`, `glassEdge`), like frosted glass. Liquid Glass (`glassEffect`) is not used: it changes its brightness with what lies behind, so panels with the same setting looked different.
  - `.popover` adds only the tint, because the popover brings its own material.
  - One surface per popup, on its root; rows get a plain highlight, never glass.
- Feature and plugin icons: `PluginIconTile(symbol:tint:size:)`, a white symbol on a tile filled with the feature's colour, `.sidebar` 20, `.list` 28, `.header` 40. Pass the feature's own `PluginTint` wherever the feature appears (sidebar, Features, setup, menu-bar popups, command bar), so it is the same tile everywhere. A tint without a colour of its own (a grey theme's accent) is drawn as a neutral tile, the control fill with an ink symbol, never a pale square that glares in dark; a pastel is deepened until the white symbol reads. Neutral, decorative symbols stay `SymbolTile`, drawn with the same proportions and corners as a `PluginIconTile` of its size.
- **Services have one mapping**: `ServiceMark.provider(_:)`, `.tool(_:)` and `.model(_:)` give every provider, coding tool and local model runtime its own symbol and tint, drawn as `PluginIconTile(mark:)` in Models, setup and pickers. No generic computer, terminal or server tile, and no two services alike. Status (installed, chosen, signed in) is a trailing `FrogBadge` or text, never in the tile's place.
- **Glyphs mean one thing**: `plus` adds, `stop.fill` stops (never a ring or dot, which read as record), `rectangle.center.inset.filled` centres a window, `pencil` edits, `ellipsis` opens more actions, `xmark` closes or removes, `arrow.up.forward` reveals in Finder (never a second folder beside a folder tile), `exclamationmark.triangle.fill` marks a failure in notices and the answer panel alike.
- **Rules wear the glyph their name says**: `SelectionBubbleItems.symbol(for:)` gives Explain its question bubble and any rule whose name says what it does its own symbol (Define a book, Translate a globe, an email an envelope, Proofread a checkmark, Summarize a list, Shorten scissors, Polish a wand); only a rule with no such word wears its feature's glyph. The bubble, the Writing rows and the command bar all ask it, so a rule looks the same in each.
- **Times have one format**, `RecentTimestamp`, as Mail writes them: "Just now", "12 min ago", the time today ("13:14"), "Yesterday", the weekday within a week, then the date ("Nov 2", with the year when it is another). In `caption`, tabular digits, one line, on the row's trailing edge, the full date and time in its tooltip; a list whose times can go stale wraps them in `TimelineView(.everyMinute)`. Hover actions that need the place take it over instead of pushing the time aside.
- **A failed answer shows only the failure**: the error and its one fix (Retry, Choose model) on the status line, with no follow-up field under it. Its header always names the model as "Model · Provider".
- **The answer panel** is header (rule, model, the pin), answer, a status line only for a failure, Stopped or the limit, the follow-up field at the panel's full width, and the shared `FrogFooter`. While the answer runs, the footer's leading side breathes (`PulseDot`) beside "Thinking…" or "Answering…" and offers Stop ⌘. and Close esc; once it is done, Copy ⌘↩, Save ⌘S (with notes on) and Close esc, each turning into what it did ("Copied", "Saved"). The answer text carries no streaming marker of its own. The field stays enabled while the answer runs, so the next question can be typed and asked with Return once it is done.
- Status pills along the bottom of the screen (Working, Done, a failure, Secure Input) are `FrogNoticePill`: a capsule that hugs its text, a symbol in the tone colour, the message, and an optional muted detail after a `·` or one compact secondary button. Working says what the rule is doing, its leading verb in the -ing form ("Proofreading…", "Translating to German…"; a name that is not a command stays as it is, "Spelling only…"), with the one ellipsis character. Done names the rule and what happened, a green `checkmark.circle.fill` and "Proofread · 3 words changed" ("No changes", "Copied"); there is no Undo in the pill, because the app the text went into keeps its own (⌘Z there). A failure, behind the warning triangle, names the rule and what went wrong first ("Proofread failed: Ollama is not running"), then a Show details button that opens the Frog window, whose banner holds the whole message; only then does the pill take clicks, otherwise it lets them through.
- **One accent.** The theme's accent is the one colour of choosing and acting: primary buttons, chosen segments, switches that are on, ticked checkboxes, active badges, focus, graph lines, the snap-zone highlight. Minimal, the default, uses indigo. `selection` (neutral) marks the chosen row in lists and the sidebar; `hover` what the pointer is over. Green (`good`) means success or granted only, never "on" or "chosen"; `warning` and `critical` are status, never decoration. No `Color.blue`, `.green` or the system accent in a view.
- **Text has two tones**: `ink` and `muted`. Every theme's `muted` keeps at least 4.5:1 on its cards, so secondary, disabled-but-informative and hint text is `muted` at full strength, never `muted.opacity(…)` below it. `placeholder` is the one lighter step, for empty fields only.

## Motion

Frog moves little: a press answers, a hover leaves softly, a choice slides, and occasional things arrive and leave. Everything else is an instant cut.

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `press` | strong ease-out, 0.14 s | `FrogButtonStyle`: scale 0.97 and the `pressed` fill on the down event; no scale under Reduce Motion or on keyboard surfaces |
| `hoverOut` | ease 0.15 s | A hover fill leaving (`frogRowHighlight`, control surfaces, buttons). Hover in is instant |
| `slide` | spring, response 0.28, no bounce | The thumb of `CompactSegments` |
| `reveal` | strong ease-out (0.23, 1, 0.32, 1), 0.2 s | Occasional arrivals: the error banner, a copy confirmation, scrolling to a section |
| `exit` | strong ease-out, 0.15 s | The same leaving, faster than it came |
| `check` | strong ease-out, 0.15 s | `FrogCheckbox`: the mark grows in from 0.8 as the box fills |
| `step` | spring, response 0.35, no bounce | Steps of the setup assistant: in 16 points from the side they lie on |
| `fadeOut` | strong ease-out, 0.2 s | Status pills and status item popups fading out (`NSWindow.fadeOut()`) |
| `resize` | ease-in-out, 0.22 s | The dictation pill growing with its text; it changes shape on screen |
| `pulse` | ease-in-out, 0.9 s, repeating | The answer panel's dot breathing while it waits; still under Reduce Motion |
| `confirmation` | 1.5 s | How long `CopyAction` shows its checkmark |

- Never animate the command bar, clipboard history, window switcher or layout picker: opening, closing, typing, arrow keys. Their roots set `.environment(\.frogKeyboardSurface, true)`, which turns off hover fades and press motion inside them.
- Never animate selection moved by arrow keys or Tab (dropdown lists set `frogKeyboardSurface` for this), page switches (sidebar and ⌘1–5), or text that streams in.
- No bounce anywhere: nothing in Frog is thrown or flicked. No ease-in: it starts slow where the eye is watching.
- Reduce Motion: no scale and no sliding; segments, setup steps, the error banner and the checkbox mark cross-fade instead.
- Copy actions use `CopyAction`: its symbol turns into a checkmark for `confirmation`.
- An animation names a token from `Motion.swift`: `withAnimation(FrogMotion.reveal)`, never `.easeOut(duration:)`, a bare `withAnimation` or a literal `NSAnimationContext` duration. `StyleTokenScanTests` checks it.

## Charts and meters

Every graph and meter is built from `FrogApp/Views/Design/FrogChart.swift`, so System monitor and Usage look like one family.

- Lines are smooth (`.monotone`, never overshooting), 2 points with round caps and joins (`FrogChart.line`). A second series is 1.5 points and uses `FrogStyle.seriesSecondary`, a soft ink that reads in every theme; the main series uses `FrogStyle.series` (the accent). Warning and critical colours colour meters only, through `FrogStyle.level`. A usage meter is the accent while it is on or behind pace, `warning` when it is ahead of pace and `critical` from 95 %; its pace text is muted unless it is ahead or the limit is reached. Never green for "under pace".
- The main line sits on a fill that fades from 35 % of its colour to clear (`FrogChart.areaFill`). Daily amounts are thin rounded `BarMark`s in the accent at low opacity.
- Minimal chrome: no axes, a few faint dashed grid lines in `hairline` (`FrogChartGrid`), and short muted labels only where they help: 0, 50 and 100 % left of the wide CPU graph (`FrogChartScaleGutter`, `FrogChart.scaleGutter` wide, the time axis indented to match), the two end dates or times under a graph (`GraphAxis`). A graph with two kinds of mark (Usage's running total over daily bars) names both in a key under it. The plot is clipped to a nested corner (`FrogChart.plotRadius`: `controlRadius` on pages, `smallRadius` in popups).
- The newest value of the main series ends in a filled dot with a soft halo (`FrogChartPoint`). Under the pointer: a thin rule (`FrogChartRule`), a dot on each line and a rounded chip (`FrogChartChip`) with the values and the time. The chip takes the corner away from the pointer, so it stays inside the plot.
- System monitor shows one window, `SystemMonitor.historyWindow` (10 minutes), in its subtitle, its axis ("10 min ago" to "Now") and its popup. The graph's card holds only what it draws: CPU and GPU, each a legend dot, its reading and a `FrogMeter`. Memory and storage share the card below, each as what is used of the total ("312 GB used of 494 GB") with its meter filled to the same share, so a figure and its bar never disagree; on the page each card has its heading (Load, Memory and storage, Power). The loading state draws the same meters' empty tracks under its skeletons. A legend dot means a line of that colour on the graph.
- Before there is a line, `FrogChartPlaceholder` shows a dashed baseline and a calm note at the graph's own height, so nothing moves when data arrives.
- `FrogMeter` is the one horizontal meter: a capsule track in `FrogStyle.track`, a fill that fades from lighter to full level colour with a soft highlight, and an optional crisp tick (the share of time gone in a plan window). Height is `.compact` (5, usage popups) or `.regular` (6, pages and the system overview). The fill glides with `FrogMotion.reveal` and does not move under Reduce Motion.
- Nothing animates continuously. Graphs are drawn from bounded data (`SystemMonitorGraph.maxPoints`) and are `Equatable`, so an unrelated update does not redraw them.
